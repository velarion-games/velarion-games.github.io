September: making the islands easier to read, and the boats worth watching

October 2026

Capture 1: a boat tacking around a headland

Where I left off

Last time I wrote that it finally feels like a game. That was true! But a game being there and a game feeling comfortable are two very different things. September had two halves: first I made the island I already had easier to understand and control, then I finally got off it.

Making it readable

Andris and Bence played the build, and they ran into the confusing parts I kind of already knew about. Watching someone else get stuck on them is different though. You can't unsee it.

So the first weeks of September went into clarity: warnings when a building can't work, tooltips, workforce and construction material displays, storage limits, and supply/demand rates so you can actually see if something runs out. Placement got better snapping, rotation and feedback, and editing grounds (fields, orchards and so on) is a lot less fiddly.

None of this is exciting to show on its own. But it's the difference between "what is going on?" and "ah, I need more bricks."

Capture 2: a building state that is readable now

New buildings from Andris

Andris delivered nine new buildings for the second population tier: Shaper housing, Brick Kiln, Weaver, Oil Press, Fig Press, Lamp Maker, Workshop, Tavern and the Boat Builder. I plugged them all into the playable build: footprints, entrances, materials, workers, unlocks. The Workshop now opens up the next tier, so the build menu grows as your town grows.

Testing them also showed me what wasn't finished behind the models, mostly needs and services. So that's what I worked on next. Thank you Andris, they look great in there!

Boats!

This was the part I was waiting for. You can build a light or a heavy cargo boat at the Boat Builder and launch it. You place a buoy, the boat finds a path around the land and sails there. It reacts to wind and current, tacks automatically when it has to go upwind, rows when there's no other way, and the sails change pose. I didn't expect how much I'd enjoy just watching it go around a headland.

Founding a second settlement

Then the boat arrives at another island. If the island has no Port yet, you can found one from the boat. In September that Port was free. Paying for it with the cargo you actually brought came next.

Capture 3: founding a new Port

This is where the game starts turning into what I wanted from the beginning. The next island is a mining island: it has iron ore, but it can't produce its own timber or staple food. Those have to come from the first island. So now the islands need each other.

What's next

The three-island economy is not done yet. Making the islands supply each other with real trade routes is the next big goal.

Thanks for reading!
